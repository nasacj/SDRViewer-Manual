What you need

Four things: a receiver, an antenna, a small computer and a Wi-Fi network. Most people already have the last two.

The list

ItemWhat it isNotes
RTL-SDR receiverA USB software-defined radio receiver, inexpensive.An RTL-SDR Blog V3, V4 or V4L is recommended: accurate and well made. A generic "TV dongle" works too.
AntennaMost receivers come with a telescopic dipole, enough for FM broadcast.For aircraft, ships and ADS-B, higher and nearer a window is better; see the tips below.
Computer or Raspberry PiMac, Windows or Linux. It only passes the receiver's samples to the network.To carry it around, a Raspberry Pi with a power bank is the easiest.
Wi-FiThe phone and that computer must be on the same network.A home router, or the phone's own Personal Hotspot.
iPhone or iPadWith SDRViewer installed.In public beta through TestFlight for now.

Why not plug the receiver into the iPhone? iOS does not let an app drive this kind of USB device. It is a limit of the system, and every app of this kind connects over the network. So one machine in between runs rtl_tcp, a small program that comes with the RTL-SDR tools.

Sample rate and the network

The receiver sends one or two million samples a second, which asks a little of the network:

UseSample rateNetwork traffic
FM broadcast, air band voice, APRS, AISabout 1 MSPSabout 16 Mbit/s
ADS-B2 MSPSabout 32 Mbit/s

Ordinary home Wi-Fi is plenty. If the audio breaks up or the app reports dropped samples, the Wi-Fi signal is usually weak: move the computer and the phone closer to the router, or put the computer on a cable.

Antenna tips

  • FM broadcast: extend each side of the dipole to about 75 cm and put it by a window.
  • Air band (118–137 MHz): about 58 cm a side, vertical, with a view of the sky or the airport.
  • ADS-B (1090 MHz): about 6.9 cm a side. This frequency travels in straight lines only; outdoors or on a window sill makes a very large difference.
  • Interference: keep the antenna away from the computer, chargers and monitors. A USB extension cable that moves the receiver away often helps.
